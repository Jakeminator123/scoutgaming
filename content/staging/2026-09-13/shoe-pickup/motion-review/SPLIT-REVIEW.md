# Split source motion verdict

Independently inspected both 73-frame 24 fps candidates at every native frame, then enlarged extract-turn n40–50 at source resolution (turn-detail.jpg). No public changes.

## Extract-turn

- n0–4: initial preparation, card starts emerging.
- n5–14: red-backed card visibly exits outlet under feeding fingers. Usable physical-extraction experiment.
- n15–34: prolonged card hold outside outlet while receiving hand approaches.
- n35–44: receiving fingers meet the card; red back remains facing camera.
- n45–48: **not a verified physical flip**. The broad red rectangle changes into a broad white face while retaining roughly the same camera-facing plane; red face details wash into queen details. The card tilts in image plane, but there is no convincingly resolved edge-on/out-of-plane turnover. This appears to be a texture morph under motion blur. It cannot pass the user's explicit physical-turn requirement.
- n49–65: face-up card lifted and presented upright while moving to centre.
- n66–72: lowered toward supplied endpoint.

The extraction n0–14 and approach n15–44 are contiguous source fragments, but no contiguous subset contains an acceptable complete extract-turn-slide action. Cutting n45–65 would conceal the central failure. New endpoint constraints reduced one failure (wrong terminal face) but did not prove the necessary card rotation.

## Slide-return

- n0–10: initial slight lift/display and lowering.
- n11–29: face-up card is flat and receiving fingers remain near trailing edge. This is the best short supported-push segment.
- n30: visible separation begins.
- n31–52: card continues slowly toward the final destination while receiving hand has already retreated; roughly 0.9 seconds of travel after separation. A real card can coast after a push, but this unusually long slow drift, changing projection and absent follow-through do not match the tightly controlled reference action. Not acceptable as the requested convincing physical placement.
- n53–72: final card settles; both hands resting. Terminal endpoint alone is correct.

n11–29 could provide a local push gesture but does not reach the final destination or include empty return. No contiguous segment supplies supported delivery through the target. Retiming cannot establish missing grip/turn or cure drifting source physics.

## Decision

**Do not promote either source as a solved physical-dealing take.** A cadence preview may be useful only if clearly labelled a rejected diagnostic, not a gameplay fix. The next image constraint must show an actual intermediate edge-on card under opposing fingers and an endpoint with the fingertips still contacting the card at the receiving destination. A face-up low endpoint alone allowed the generator to morph paper and animate detached drift. Review that intermediate image for anatomy and actual opposing grip before another motion job.
